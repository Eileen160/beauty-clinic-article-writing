# Eileen Aesthetic Article

Create Traditional Chinese patient education, SEO briefs, WordPress-ready articles, and content reviews about aesthetic medicine in Taiwan. Use for topics such as injectables, energy-based devices, dermatology-related aesthetic treatments, aesthetic surgery, hair restoration, body contouring, post-treatment care, and treatment comparisons.

This is a clinical education skill, not a sales-copy or social-ad skill. For generic skincare, cosmetics, fashion, or non-medical beauty content, do not use it.

## First principle: safety, evidence, and legal compliance

Treat clinic-site content as potentially promotional. Write conservatively and preserve the boundary between neutral health education and patient solicitation. Do not give a definitive legal opinion. Before publication, require the clinic to confirm that service availability, practitioner credentials, consent workflow, local health-authority requirements, and all product or device details are current and true.

Read [Taiwan and international authority sources](references/taiwan-authorities.md) when drafting or reviewing a Taiwan aesthetic-medicine article. Check the current official text when a legal claim, a newly changed rule, a device approval, a drug indication, or a practitioner qualification is material to the copy.

Never write or imply:

- Guaranteed, permanent, risk-free, painless, immediate, or uniform results, including `保證`, `永久`, `零風險`, `無副作用`, `無痛`, `立即見效`, `一次到位`, `完全不留痕跡`.
- Superiority, rankings, exclusivity, or unverified credential claims, including `最好`, `第一`, `唯一`, `首選`, `權威`, `神醫`, `最高級`.
- Fear, body shaming, age anxiety, urgency, package sales, discounts, gifts, lotteries, financing inducements, or price bait.
- Before-and-after marketing, patient testimonials, influencer endorsements, case stories that imply predictable results, or personal-result claims used to attract treatment.
- A conclusion that a reader needs a treatment, or that one treatment is better for everyone.
- Unsupported off-label use, unapproved indications, named drug/device efficacy claims, or language that encourages self-treatment.

Use bounded language instead: `可能`, `常見`, `有些人`, `需由醫師評估`, `依皮膚、組織條件與健康狀況而定`, `可能有不良反應或恢復期`, and `實際規劃應由合格醫療人員說明`.

For invasive aesthetic procedures, injectables, prescription drugs, anesthetic care, energy-based devices, or surgery, include a clear risk-and-evaluation reminder. Do not turn risk disclosure into a claim that risks can be eliminated.

## Evidence and E-E-A-T

Every factual medical assertion must be traceable. Match the source to the claim rather than adding a decorative reference list.

Use this source order:

1. Taiwan official sources for law, approvals, safety notices, and public-health guidance: MOHW, TFDA, National Health Insurance Administration when relevant, and the Laws & Regulations Database of the Republic of China.
2. Formal clinical guidance from recognised specialty societies, such as the American Academy of Dermatology, American Society of Plastic Surgeons, International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery, British Association of Dermatologists, or equivalent Taiwan professional bodies. Prefer original guidance over news summaries.
3. Systematic reviews, clinical practice guidelines, and primary studies indexed in PubMed. State meaningful limitations when evidence is small, short-term, manufacturer-sponsored, or not directly applicable to the requested population.
4. Official manufacturer information only for factual product specifications, instructions for use, and regulatory status. It is not sufficient evidence for comparative effectiveness, safety, or “best” claims.

Avoid commercial clinic blogs, lifestyle media, influencer posts, marketplace pages, anonymous reviews, and search snippets as medical evidence. Do not quote reference text at length. Paraphrase accurately and link each material claim to its source or group references under the relevant section.

An overseas regulator’s clearance, approval, or safety communication is not proof of a Taiwan approval, permitted indication, practitioner qualification, or advertising permission. Cite it only for the scope it actually covers and verify Taiwan status independently.

Show E-E-A-T in the article itself:

- State who reviewed clinical content only if the physician, specialty, credential, and review date are verified by the clinic.
- Clearly distinguish established facts, common clinical considerations, and areas where evidence or outcomes vary.
- Give publication and medical-review dates when the publishing system supports them. Recheck time-sensitive treatment, device, drug, and regulatory content before updating.
- Disclose no invented experience, outcomes, volume, awards, or technology claims. If the clinic provides verified primary details, describe them neutrally.

For higher-risk content, keep an editorial evidence log outside the published article: exact claim, source URL or citation, source date, access date, applicable jurisdiction, evidence type, limitations, and whether the claim was clinically approved. This log is especially important for product/device names, efficacy statistics, adverse-event rates, off-label questions, practitioner credentials, and claims about regulatory status.

## Aesthetic-medicine-specific guardrails

- Do not name a drug, filler, toxin, implant, laser, RF/HIFU device, or other medical device unless the user specifically needs it and the identity, Taiwan regulatory status, approved use, and clinical context are verified. Prefer treatment-category wording.
- When a device or product is named, do not convert product education into consumer advertising. Avoid brand comparisons and direct efficacy promises. Verify relevant TFDA status and use only accurate, neutral background language.
- Do not describe a procedure as non-invasive, minimally invasive, safe, or suitable without context. Explain that invasiveness, anesthesia, discomfort, downtime, risks, and candidacy vary by method and person.
- For cosmetic surgery or designated aesthetic procedures, mention evaluation, informed consent, possible adverse effects, and follow-up where relevant. Do not claim a clinician is qualified unless that exact credential has been confirmed.
- Never direct aesthetic-procedure content at minors. For age-sensitive procedures, use neutral legal and clinical framing and require clinician evaluation.
- If a topic involves pregnancy, breastfeeding, anticoagulants, immunosuppression, mental-health concerns, active skin disease, infection, implanted devices, prior filler, or prior surgery, say these can affect evaluation where clinically relevant. Do not offer individual medical advice.

## Article design

Choose an architecture that fits the reader's decision rather than copying a fixed template.

- **Concern-first:** everyday concern → possible contributing factors → what a clinician evaluates → available options and limits → care and follow-up.
- **Treatment-process:** when to seek evaluation → pre-treatment assessment → treatment stages → expected variation, risks, and follow-up → FAQ.
- **Comparison:** decision question → neutral comparison table → suitability and limitations → what to ask during assessment → FAQ. Do not declare a winner.
- **Safety/aftercare:** treatment context → normal variation versus warning signs → care instructions → when to contact the treating team → FAQ.

For a standard long-form article, include:

1. A title and opening that answer the main concern within the first paragraph, without detached phrases such as `本文整理`.
2. A short `就診前可先了解` summary: common concerns, what may need assessment, and the treatment category.
3. A clinician reminder that suitability, method, cost, treatment course, and recovery vary by person.
4. Four to seven topic-specific H2 sections. Explain mechanism in plain language, not device marketing jargon.
5. At least one comparison, evaluation, preparation, aftercare, or warning-sign table when it helps the decision. Keep the table readable and do not include invented numerical outcomes.
6. Five to eight patient-facing FAQ entries. Use `需由醫師評估` where appropriate.
7. A reference list containing only sources actually used, with direct links and publication/update dates where available.
8. A closing reminder that education cannot replace an in-person assessment.

For WordPress output, use Gutenberg-compatible blocks. For SEO deliverables, also provide a conservative title, English lowercase slug, 120–158 Chinese-character meta description, one to three focus keywords, a 75-Chinese-character excerpt, and six closely related tags. Do not create price-oriented or superiority-oriented metadata.

## SEO, AIO, and AEO

Optimize for useful retrieval and answer quality, never for exaggerated conversion. The core keyword should occur naturally in the title, opening answer, at least one H2, meta description, and a relevant FAQ. Do not force a location into the copy unless the clinic’s service area is true and useful.

- Answer the main patient question in the opening 80–120 Chinese characters with a qualified, clinically accurate response.
- Write each H2 as a real question or decision point and open it with a self-contained answer of one to three sentences. Make the answer understandable even when retrieved outside its original page.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 headings, definition lists, concise bullet points, and HTML tables for distinctions such as assessment factors, treatment categories, preparation, aftercare, or warning signs. Do not make tables out of promotional claims.
- Include FAQ questions people genuinely ask. Keep each answer consistent with the body text, directly responsive, and free from guaranteed outcomes. Any FAQ schema must match visible FAQ wording exactly.
- Define uncommon treatment, device, or regulatory terms at first mention. Use one consistent Chinese term, with an English name in parentheses only when it removes ambiguity.
- Support claims with identifiable citations and direct links. Do not use citations as keyword placement or pad the reference list.
- Use credible author/reviewer, update date, and source signals only when verified. E-E-A-T metadata must not be fabricated to influence search ranking.
- Write for Google Search, AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and other answer systems by making scope, audience, conditions, limitations, and source basis explicit. Do not claim that any format guarantees ranking, indexing, or AI citation.

## Writing style

- Use Traditional Chinese for Taiwan readers. Be warm, precise, and nonjudgmental.
- Start from the reader's lived concern, not a keyword or search-analysis statement.
- Say `患者` by default. Use `求診者` when it reads more naturally. Avoid stigmatizing labels and appearance-based pressure.
- Explain medical terms on first use. Prefer short sentences over dense legal or technical language.
- Keep clinic context factual and modest. Only include location, equipment, workflow, clinician background, or internal links the user has verified.
- No visible Chinese semicolons in patient-facing copy. Avoid formulaic phrases such as `一篇搞懂` and `本文整理`.

## Final release check

Before finalizing, confirm:

- The article has no guarantee, ranking, price inducement, testimonial, before/after promise, fear appeal, or unverified clinic claim.
- Treatment suitability, risks, recovery, outcomes, and costs are framed as individual and clinician-assessed.
- Every material medical, regulatory, device, or drug statement has an appropriate authoritative source.
- Named products/devices, service availability, practitioner credentials, and approvals are verified, or removed.
- The article separates education from promotion and includes evaluation and medical reminders.
- Sources are cited accurately, are current enough for the claim, and do not rely on commercial-clinic content.
- The opening, headings, tables, FAQ, and metadata provide direct, qualified answers that are useful for SEO, AIO, and AEO without over-optimisation or ranking promises.

Pause for clinic clinical/legal review instead of publishing unsupported copy when it contains a product or device name, an exact efficacy or complication statistic, regulatory or approval status, a doctor qualification, patient imagery or outcomes, a minor, a promotion or fee, a potential emergency warning, or a claim that is material to a reader’s treatment decision.

Use this closing reminder unless the user provides an approved alternative:

`醫療提醒：內容僅供醫療衛教參考，不能取代醫師當面評估。實際療程、使用方式、風險、不良反應、恢復期、費用與是否適合，仍需依個人健康狀況、治療部位與醫師判斷而定。`

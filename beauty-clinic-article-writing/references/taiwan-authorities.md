# Taiwan and international aesthetic-medicine authority sources

Use this reference only for Taiwanese aesthetic-medicine health education and regulatory/compliance work. Links are official starting points, not a substitute for checking the current text and local implementation before publication. Use each source only for claims within its jurisdiction and expertise.

## Regulatory baseline

- [Laws & Regulations Database of the Republic of China — Medical Care Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=L0020021): check the definition of medical advertising and the current rules on its permitted and prohibited forms. Treat online clinic content as potentially promotional when it is intended to solicit medical services.
- [MOHW — medical advertising management](https://dep.mohw.gov.tw/Doma/fp-2708-38120-106.html): official portal for medical-advertising notices and interpretations. Use as a routing source and verify the underlying current notice.
- [Regulations Governing the Management of the Review and Approval of the Application for Specific Medical Techniques, Examinations, Tests and Medical Devices](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?PCODE=L0020075): check current requirements relevant to cosmetic medical surgery and designated aesthetic medical procedures, including institutional, practitioner, consent, reporting, and record-keeping requirements.
- [TFDA — medical-device advertising](https://www.fda.gov.tw/TC/site.aspx?r=1391416143&sid=1993): use for current medical-device advertising guidance and related information.
- [TFDA — medical-device regulations](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/includes/GetFile.ashx?cid=9425&id=f638411084356693490): use as a legal reference route. Verify the current statute and any product-specific status independently.

## Claim-specific research routes

- [TFDA](https://www.fda.gov.tw/): Taiwan device and drug safety notices, regulatory information, and product-related verification routes.
- [Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.gov.tw/): medical policy, safety communication, and health guidance.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/): systematic reviews, guidelines, and original clinical evidence. Check study type, population, comparator, follow-up, conflicts, and applicability.
- [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/): systematic reviews when available and applicable.

## International regulators and public-health authorities

- [U.S. FDA — Aesthetic (Cosmetic) Devices](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices): device-risk, intended-use, clearance/approval, and patient-safety background. FDA status is not Taiwan TFDA status and must not be described as such.
- [U.S. FDA — Medical Device Databases](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/medical-device-databases): use a specific record only when it matches the exact product, indication, and regulatory question.
- [European Medicines Agency](https://www.ema.europa.eu/): medicine safety information and European regulatory context. Do not treat an EMA decision as a Taiwan drug indication.
- [UK Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/medicines-and-healthcare-products-regulatory-agency): UK safety alerts and medical-product regulation. Use for UK context only.
- [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/): evidence-based UK guidance and evidence summaries where a relevant topic is available. Check whether the guidance applies to cosmetic rather than reconstructive or disease treatment.
- [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/): patient-facing safety and care information. Prefer specialty guidance or systematic evidence for comparative efficacy claims.

## International specialty bodies

- [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/): dermatology and procedural skin-care guidance, patient safety, and condition-specific information.
- [American Society for Dermatologic Surgery](https://www.asds.net/): dermatologic-surgery and procedural guidance. Prefer formal statements, guidelines, or peer-reviewed material over marketing pages.
- [American Society of Plastic Surgeons](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/for-medical-professionals/health-policy/practice-references): plastic-surgery practice references and patient-safety material. Verify topic-specific applicability.
- [International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery](https://www.isaps.org/education/publications/patient-safety-guidance-library/): international aesthetic-plastic-surgery safety guidance and informed-consent resources.
- [European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology](https://eadv.org/): dermatology guidance and educational resources, particularly for skin disease and procedural dermatology.
- [British Association of Dermatologists](https://www.bad.org.uk/): patient information and clinical guidance in dermatology. Use only current, topic-relevant material.

## Evidence quality and study appraisal

When a topic lacks a formal guideline, prefer systematic reviews and well-designed primary studies over single-centre case series or manufacturer marketing. Record the publication date, population, treatment protocol, comparator, follow-up duration, adverse-event reporting, funding, and conflicts of interest before turning findings into patient-facing copy.

- [EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/): reporting-guideline directory, useful for judging whether a study reports methods and outcomes transparently.
- [PRISMA](https://www.prisma-statement.org/): reporting standards for systematic reviews. It supports appraisal of reporting quality but does not by itself establish treatment effectiveness.
- [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/): trial registry for locating study protocols and status. A registered or completed trial is not proof that a treatment is effective, approved, or suitable.

## How to cite

For legal or approval claims, cite the specific official legal text, notice, or TFDA record that supports it and record the access date in editorial notes. For treatment facts, cite the relevant guideline or research directly near the claim or in a clearly mapped reference section. Do not cite an official organization merely because its domain is authoritative if the page does not support the assertion. Do not transpose a foreign regulator’s clearance, approval, label, or safety communication into a Taiwan legal or marketing claim.

If sources conflict, are outdated, or do not support the requested marketing claim, remove or soften the claim and flag it for clinical/legal review rather than resolving it with speculation.
